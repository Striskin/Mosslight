# Mosslight: The Quiet Bell

A small, original medieval woodland action RPG in C++17 and raylib 5.5. Restore a forgotten
shrine, explore a knight-held bailey, trade in village shops, and return the valley's song. All pixel artwork and
sound cues are generated by the game from original shapes and tones. No external
art assets, engine, ECS framework, scripting runtime, or additional game library.

## Windows: quick build

Install the **MSYS2 UCRT64** toolchain. In the MSYS2 UCRT64 terminal:

```sh
pacman -S --needed mingw-w64-ucrt-x86_64-gcc
```

Add `C:\msys64\ucrt64\bin` to your Windows PATH, then open PowerShell in this folder:

```powershell
powershell -NoProfile -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -File scripts\build.ps1 -Test -Smoke
.\build\Mosslight.exe
```

The script downloads a pinned official raylib 5.5 MinGW release on the first build,
checks its SHA-256, compiles the game and tests, copies data and the raylib license,
and updates `Whats_This.txt` with the actual result. `-Run` opens the game after
building. No CMake is needed for this route. The result statically links raylib
and the GCC runtime, so distribute `Mosslight.exe`, `data/`, and `RAYLIB-LICENSE.txt`
together. No MSYS2 installation is needed on the receiving Windows PC.

### CMake / Visual Studio alternative

Install Visual Studio's Desktop development with C++ workload and CMake. From a
Developer PowerShell prompt:

```powershell
cmake -S . -B build-vs -A x64
cmake --build build-vs --config Release
ctest --test-dir build-vs -C Release --output-on-failure
.\build-vs\Release\Mosslight.exe
```

CMake uses an installed raylib package or downloads raylib 5.5 source. Windows,
Linux, and macOS code paths are provided, but only the Windows MinGW build has
been exercised here. CMake builds do not automatically edit the project memory;
update `Whats_This.txt` after using that route.

The game pauses when it loses focus. Dialogue, menus, inventory, journal and
shop screens pause combat. Resize freely: the 480x270 canvas uses integer scaling and black
letterboxing. Window size cannot be smaller than the virtual resolution.

## Your first journey

1. Talk to **Keeper Aven**, beside the village square. Open the welcome chest.
2. Go east into **Fernwake Wood**. Its eastern cache holds the **Ember Seed**.
3. Head south to **Stillwater Cave**. Find **Moon Dew** in its southeast chamber.
4. Optional: explore the forest's northwest **Wandering Hollow**. Its far southeast
   room has a **Copper blade**, which doubles sword damage. Room templates,
   connecting paths, encounters, and supply loot use your saved world seed.
5. Trade four glow fragments with **Talla the Weaver** in Hearthmere for a coat.
   It adds two maximum hearts. New armor upgrades equip on first acquisition;
   select an owned coat or plate in the satchel and press Enter to change it.
6. Go north from the forest to **The Quiet Bell Shrine**, offer the relics at its
   bowl, then rest at the shrine lantern. The northern seal is now open.
7. Face the **Hollow Warden**. Amber lines telegraph a charge, rings telegraph a
   volley. Its attacks accelerate below half health. Dodge, then strike during
   recovery. You may retreat south. Return to Aven after victory for the ending.

Lanterns restore all hearts and stamina, set your respawn checkpoint, and save.
**Resting or respawning resets encounters and clears unclaimed ground loot.**
Death returns you to the last lantern with your coins, inventory and quest intact.
Defeated enemies and their unclaimed loot persist across travel and save/load
until you rest. Living enemies reload at full health. A defeated boss stays defeated.
Opened chests stay open.
There is no penalty or time limit, and no required grind for the relic quest.

## Steel & Hearth expansion (0.2)

**You can continue your old save.** The game migrates the original format and
gives existing travellers the same initial **24 coins and 12 arrows** as new ones.
No restart is required, even if you already completed the Quiet Bell quest.

### Shops and places

- **The Ember & Anvil:** northeast village house, marked **SMITH**. Stand at its
  door and press E, then talk to Master Harl. He sells an ashwood bow (18 coins),
  ten arrows (4 coins), knight longsword (32 coins), and knight plate (38 coins).
- **The Copper Kettle:** northwest village house, marked **INN**. Nessa sells
  tonics and arrows. The guest hearth is a free rest/save/respawn checkpoint.
- **Greywatch Bailey:** continue east through the forest. Guarded knights patrol
  this fortified courtyard. A chest beyond them contains knight plate; an
  archer's cache holds spare arrows. A roadwarden and lantern wait outside.
- **Wandering residents:** seeded visitors stroll around the village and inn,
  stop near you, and share practical lore. Friendly knights explain combat.

### Combat tactics

- **Fists (1):** quick, low stamina cost, short reach. L performs a strong shove.
- **Sword (2):** balanced reach, with a visible windup, active strike and recovery.
  The copper blade deals 2 damage; the knight longsword deals 3. L adds 2 damage
  and breaks a knight's frontal guard, but commits you to a slower strike.
- **Bow (3):** buy it first. Hold RMB to aim and J to fire. Each shot costs one
  arrow and stamina. Arrows collide with walls, hit one target, and can be stopped
  by a knight's shield. Fire during an opening or from behind.
- **Guard (K):** protects the direction you face with fists or sword equipped.
  A block costs stamina. Catch a blow in the first 0.16 seconds to stagger the
  attacker; attacks during stagger deal +1 damage. An exhausted guard breaks.
  RMB lets you turn your guard while strafing. You cannot guard with a bow.
- **Stamina:** the green bar fuels attacks, blocking, sprinting and dodging.
  Release your attacks to recover. You cannot cancel a committed attack into a
  dodge or block. Leave yourself enough stamina to escape.
- **Knight plate:** visibly replaces your outfit, grants 10 maximum hearts and
  reduces hits of 2+ damage by 1. Movement is 9% slower and dodging costs more.
  Strongest owned swords equip automatically. First-time armor upgrades equip;
  the satchel now lets you change armor or return to travel clothes. Rest to fill new hearts.

### Tactical controls update (0.3)

- **F or middle mouse** focuses the nearest nearby enemy with a clear line of
  sight. WASD now strafes while you keep facing it. **R** cycles targets, **F**
  releases, and **RMB** switches back to manual aim. Lock-on assists facing for
  melee, directional guard and bow shots; attacks still have their normal windup.
  A gold reticle marks your target and the camera frames both combatants. Focus
  ends on death, blocked sight, distance, or a region change. It never fires for you.
- **Choose armor:** open **Tab**, scroll to an owned coat or plate with **W/S**,
  then press **Enter** to wear it. Enter on worn armor removes it. Travel clothes
  give 6 hearts, the coat gives 8, and plate gives 10 plus heavy-hit protection.
  Clothes/coat keep full speed and 22-stamina dodges; plate uses 91% speed and
  28-stamina dodges. The satchel shows these values and the worn outfit changes
  visibly. Lowering capacity clamps health; swapping back never heals you.
  Talla still sells a light coat after you own plate. You cannot change gear
  during a committed attack, dodge or stun.
- **F1 / H** opens a two-page field guide from the title or during a journey.
  Use W/S to switch between controls and combat advice. It pauses simulation,
  then returns to the same menu or unfinished dialogue. Also available in pause.

### Coins and searchable remains

Defeat creatures or knights, approach their remains, and press **E to search**.
Loot is physical and persists in saves. Ironback beetles leave shells and coins;
other enemies leave glow fragments and coins. Merchants pay **6 coins per shell**
and **2 per fragment**. Use W/S and Enter in their trade screen. Quest relics cannot
be sold. Failed purchases never charge coins, and full bags retain leftover loot.

## Saves

On Windows: `%LOCALAPPDATA%\Mosslight\journey.sav`. One save slot. New journey asks
before replacing it. Autosaves occur at new game, region transitions, enemy defeats,
searched/chest loot, merchant trades, quest changes, lanterns, respawn, and clean
exit. F5 and the pause menu also save.

Saves include region, position, health, stamina, inventory/equipped weapon and armor, coins,
quest flags, opened chest IDs, checkpoint, elapsed play time, cave seed, defeated
spawn IDs, and uncollected loot for each region. Version 3 reads versions 1 and 2,
keeping their previous strongest armor equipped. Your explicit armor choice then
persists, including choosing a coat while carrying plate. Target focus is transient.
The format is
versioned, size-limited, value-validated, checksummed, and atomically replaced.
A valid previous save is retained as `.bak` and recovered if the primary fails.
The checksum detects accidental corruption, not intentional save editing.

```powershell
.\build\Mosslight.exe --save .\build\my-test.sav --mute
.\build\Mosslight.exe --smoke-test --captures .\build\captures
```

Smoke mode uses a separate save and never touches your normal journey. It renders
all regions and key screens, captures PNGs, exercises respawn and load, and exits.

## Code and data

| Module | Responsibility |
| --- | --- |
| `main` | raylib lifetime, input, focus, command line, visual smoke harness |
| `game` | screens, interactions, quest orchestration, one active region |
| `player`, `collision` | movement, facing, dodge, safe swept tile movement |
| `enemies`, `combat` | distinct AI, boss phases, projectiles, sword hits, damage |
| `inventory`, `dialogue` | items, armor choices, sword upgrades, conversations and objectives |
| `targeting` | stable enemy focus, line-of-sight/range checks, target cycling and aim |
| `merchant`, `npcs` | validated buy/sell transactions and seeded walking residents |
| `maps`, `procedural` | strict map parsing, deterministic connected template cave |
| `save` | validated snapshots, atomic persistence and backup |
| `render`, `ui`, `audio` | original pixel art, camera, screens, generated sounds |
| `tests/tests.cpp`, `tests/expansion.hpp`, `tests/controls.hpp` | simulation, combat, economy, controls, migration and persistence regression checks |

`data/maps/*.map` are handcrafted ASCII grids with explicit exits, objects, and
enemy spawns. `scripts/author-maps.ps1` is an optional offline authoring utility;
the runtime never runs it. `data/rooms.txt` contains 11x9 cave templates.
`data/items.txt`, `data/enemies.txt`, and `data/shops.txt` define item descriptions,
resale values, enemy tuning, and merchant offers.

Tiles: `.` grass/floor, `+` trail, `,` flowers, `:` shrine stone, `_` bridge,
`*` crystals, `T` solid trees, `#` solid rock, `~` solid deep water,
`H` solid house footprint, `P` solid pillar, `b` barrels, `=` counter/table,
`f` forge/hearth, `s` shelving (all solid), `r` rug, `B` banner (walkable).
Objects use tile-center coordinates. A `door` record specifies an interior and
arrival tile; merchants are object kind 6.

## Scope and next milestone

This is a playable slice, not a full-length RPG. Nine connected regions, two
enterable businesses, one quest chain, four regular enemy types, a two-phase
boss, three combat styles, an equipment/economy loop, a seeded cave and a short
ending. The remaining village cottage is scenery. No controller support,
remapping, multiple save slots or authored soundtrack yet. Swords still use the
strongest owned upgrade; armor is selectable. Enemies use local steering,
not pathfinding; visitors have simple wandering behavior rather than schedules.

Most valuable next milestone: human playtests of stamina, shield timing, bow feel,
knight encounters and merchant prices, then tune the economy and combat before
adding more content. Automated checks do not replace a human playthrough.
Read `Whats_This.txt` before editing and update it after code work and builds.

and [API reference](https://www.raylib.com/cheatsheet/cheatsheet.html).
